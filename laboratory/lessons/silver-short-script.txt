In front of you is silver nitrate solution, containing thirty grams of silver. You cannot see it: the silver is dissolved as ions. That is quite a lot of money. So, how do we get it back?

Table salt provides chloride. Add it, and the equilibrium shifts right. Silver chloride forms a solid. The silver was here all along.

But silver chloride is not something you take to the pawn shop. We want silver metal. Oxidation state zero.

Back to our original solution. This Pourbaix diagram shows pH across and potential up. Higher means more oxidizing.

Silver ions above this line. Silver metal below. But a dot supplies no electrons. We need a donor.

Copper’s boundary lies lower. Add copper powder. Copper gives up electrons, and silver ions become silver metal.

Now we have fine silver dust and chunks, and copper ions in solution. Could we collect silver directly on an electrode instead?

Take a fresh silver solution. Beside it, copper ions and a copper electrode. Connect a salt bridge and wire. Copper dissolves; silver plates onto our silver electrode.

Platinum can support silver plating too. The silver couple’s equilibrium potential stays the same. Starting deposition on platinum may need extra overpotential. Same chemistry, different surface. Bergslagen Chemicals.
