This clear beaker contains thirty grams of silver. You cannot see it, because the silver is dissolved as ions. So, how do we get it back?

Add chloride, and watch. Silver chloride forms a solid. The silver was here all along. Chemical equilibrium tells us how much stays dissolved.

But silver chloride is not exactly something you take to the pawn shop. We want metallic silver. Oxidation state zero.

Return to our original silver solution. Now pull back. This is a Pourbaix diagram: pH across, electric potential up. Higher means more oxidizing conditions.

Up here, silver ions stay dissolved. Move below the boundary, and metallic silver is stable. The beaker follows the calculated equilibrium. But moving a dot supplies no electrons.

Copper can. Its metal to ion boundary lies below silver’s. Copper gives up electrons; silver ions accept them. Watch copper powder enter, and silver metal form.
