Source-based chromium/iron equilibrium records from the factory task. Reduce Cr(VI) with Fe(II), then collect mixed Cr(III)/Fe(III) hydroxides. The precipitation sequence is replayed with overlaid dissolved Cr(III), Fe(III), and excess Fe(II) curves. Each curve sums all dissolved species of that oxidation state, not just the free ion. The same Fe/Cr electron inventory is maintained at each pH. The selected pH 8.8 maximizes chromium recovery on the sampled pH 2–14 grid (0.1 pH increments); it is a model optimum, not a universal treatment prescription. The solid collection animation illustrates settling/separation. Verify treated wastewater against plant conditions before discharge. Cr(III) is less toxic than Cr(VI), not harmless. No calcium/carbonate sequence appears in this edition.

The separate platinum half-cells illustrate Fe(II) oxidation coupled to Cr(VI) reduction, not chromium metal plating. At pH 2, a representative balanced cathode reaction is HCrO4- + 7H+ + 3e- -> Cr3+ + 4H2O; the anode is Fe2+ -> Fe3+ + e-. Equal nominal solvent inventories; 0.032 mol/kg total Fe and 0.010 mol/kg total Cr, initially 99% Fe(II) and 99% Cr(VI). The initial ideal EMF is calculated from the source speciation model, with finite minority redox inventories. Both cells use Pt and a spectator-ion bridge. Ideal activities, 25 C; sulfate complexation and kinetics omitted. The precipitation reference uses bundled Cr2O3(cr), log formation constant -15.48, and includes Fe(OH)2(cr) alongside Fe(OH)3(am). Its Cr(III) minimum is approximately 3.74e-9 mol/kg. This oxide reference is not a prediction that fresh hydroxide instantly becomes crystalline oxide; fresh precipitate solubility may be higher. Coexisting iron solids conserve both metal and electron inventories. The cell EMF updates from approximately 0.500 to 0.336 V as reaction extent changes; it is not a loaded terminal-voltage calculation. Arrows, colour changes, electrons, and lamp brightness are teaching illustrations, not calculated currents, rates, or light output. The Pt setup is a thermodynamic illustration, not a validated industrial treatment design.
